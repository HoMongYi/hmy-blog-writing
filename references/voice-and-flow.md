# Voice, Rhythm, and Paragraph Flow

## 1. Personal Blog Voice

Use the candid voice of a personal blog, adapting the mood to its reader and purpose. Firsthand narration and private feelings or beliefs require user-provided material. Reflective writing need not sound like a technical guide, and technical writing need not sound like a report.

- **Base register:** Natural Korean plain declarative (`~다`체), written as a candid personal record and practical guide—never as a corporate report, wiki entry, or translated manual.
- **Strict Boundary When Replacing Repetitive Advice:**
  - Do not end paragraph after paragraph with advisory formulas such as `~하는 편이 좋다`, `~부터 확인하면 된다`, `~를 함께 봐야 한다`, or `~하는 게 낫다`.
  - **CRITICAL WARNING — Never convert general advice into an unconfirmed first-person habit or past action:**
    - Synthetic example: '설정 파일의 사본이 있으면 변경 전 상태와 비교할 수 있다' must not become '나는 매번 설정 파일을 복사해 둔다' without evidence of that habit. Keep the general explanation or use the user's confirmed action.
- **Natural Reactions Without Quota:**
  - Preserve user-supplied reactions (`ㅋㅋ`, `ㅎㅎ`, `엥?`, emoji, short sighs) where they fit.
  - In Research Writing, light commentary grounded in verified facts is welcome. Distinguish reasoned interpretation from an unconfirmed personal preference, feeling, or history. Do not insert reactions or jokes to meet a quota.

## 2. Meaning-Driven Paragraph Breathing

Do not enforce mechanical rules such as '2–3 sentences per paragraph' or '100–150 characters.' Judge paragraphs by thought unit and visual rhythm on the intended desktop and mobile layouts. Distinguish semantic paragraph boundaries from wrapping caused by width, font, and CSS; a Markdown line break is not necessarily a new HTML paragraph.

- **When to split a paragraph:**
  1. **Layer shift:** When moving from a scene or experience to interpretation, explanation, a condition, or a caution.
  2. **Contrast or turn:** When the topic, viewpoint, time, or situation changes. A connective word alone does not require a new paragraph.
  3. **Standalone emphasis:** A single sentence that lands a clear takeaway or transition may stand alone.
- **When to merge or keep together:**
  - Keep closely connected cause-and-effect sentences in one paragraph rather than chopping every sentence into a one-liner.

## 3. Anti-Meta Narration & Custom Excerpt

- Never narrate internal verification rules or defensive disclaimers (such as announcing that the paragraph passed an editorial checklist) in public prose.
- **Excerpt:** Connect the real motivation or question to the post without listing editorial procedures. Follow a destination field's actual limit if supplied; do not impose a fixed sentence count or a universal length rule.

## 4. Deliberate Non-Change (Over-Rewriting Guard)

- When editing or rewriting an existing post, **do not rewrite sentences that already read naturally and state the facts clearly.**
